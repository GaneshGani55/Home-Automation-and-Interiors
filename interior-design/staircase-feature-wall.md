## 1. Where things stand

Two folded-plate RCC flights (a *folded plate* = a stair cast as one zig-zag concrete slab, no central wall) rise through the double-height living: flight 1 runs west along this 3.08 m wall from the stair base to the half landing at +1.76 m, flight 2 crosses back east in front of it to the first floor, and the terrace flight sits directly over flight 1. Fixed on the wall: the Waveshare touch panel (cavity already cut, centre 1500 mm), three foot lamps, no switchboard, no strip lights, one aimable spot over the landing, and three fresh core cuts in the underside of the terrace flight for accent lights.

Why the lighting map showed no ambient or accent symbols on 2026-10-11: a drawing bug in the map (fixed and republished the same day, commit 54f9bc3), not a design decision. Design-wise, this wall did have only *task* light (foot lamps + the landing spot) and borrowed its ambient from the living (pendant cluster, the 24 V line under the edge beams, the Axios spill, ≈ 150 lx in the FULL scene). Your three core cuts are the missing *accent* layer; this document adds it.

---

## 2. The pick — "Ivory Comb" with two grafts

**Make the whole wall, floor to roof slab, one field of warm-ivory plaster cut into 11 flat vertical bands by 10 sharp shadow grooves (20 mm wide × 10 mm deep); set the Waveshare into it as a walnut-lined niche; put the three foot lamps and the three soffit spots on two parallel diagonals that climb with the stair; keep the landing light as the aimable Avia and move it to the side wall if the landing turns out to be open to the terrace landing above.** Same ivory as the rest of the house — the relief is the feature, not a colour.

It won the three-judge review on combined score (Comb 21 · Travertine + walnut 18 · Linen gallery 16.5): the coherence judge gave it 8/10 (the only concept that keeps the palette's "one feature wall per contiguous space" rule and is literally the finish both renders you liked and the lighting design already assume), the buildability judge 7/10 (cheapest, every Chitradurga mason and painter can do it, a hand-mark is a ₹2k repaint), and the lighting judge 6/10 with one hard objection — vertical grooves lit from straight above throw no shadow. That objection is fixed below by turning the spots along the wall instead of straight down, and the two grafts take the best of the losers: the walnut-lined panel niche from the travertine concept and the Avia-on-a-plate accent fixture from the gallery concept.

## 3. The wall, element by element

### 3.1 Finish — grooved ivory plaster

Plain words first. A *render/skim* is a thin new plaster layer over the existing wall that gives a dead-flat surface. A *groove profile* ("groove patti" / "shadow-gap bead") is a U-shaped aluminium strip you bury in the plaster so the groove comes out straight and sharp — a mason cannot cut 65 m of perfectly straight slots by hand.

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Base | Existing cement plaster checked TRUE with a 2 m straightedge (≤ 3 mm wave). Grazing light shows every ripple, so straightness is the whole job. |
| Grooves | 10 vertical aluminium profiles 20 mm wide × 10 mm deep, square-edged, dead plumb (laser line), fixed with polymer dabs. Pitch ≈ 280 mm, bands ≈ 260 mm — **compute from the measured wall length** L: band = (L − 10 × 20 mm) / 11. |
| New layer | 10–12 mm polymer-modified cement render (1:4 with SBR/acrylic bonding admixture) built flush to the profile faces, then 2 coats acrylic wall putty sanded flat. **Not gypsum** on this wall: the GF common bath's shower valve and cistern are on the other face of this 230 mm wall and gypsum blisters if it ever sweats. |
| Movement joints | A 5 mm cut slot along every line where RCC meets brick (flight-1 soffit, terrace-flight soffit, landing slab edges). The render *will* crack there — give it a slot to crack in; the colour change (soft-snow soffit → ivory wall) sits on the same line. |
| Colour | Bands AND grooves the same Warm Ivory #F2EBDD — Asian Paints 7942 White Sand (alt 0125 Ivory Touch), Royale Matt or equivalent washable matte. Never paint the grooves darker (a painted shadow looks fake the moment real light arrives); never sheen (it flashes under grazing light). |
| Extent | One continuous field from the floor skirting to the underside of the roof slab (≈ 6.5 m) with no break at FF level: behind flight 1, across the landing back wall, up Bed 2's north face behind the terrace flight. Includes the low under-flight-1 triangle (seen from the hall). Stops at the exposed east corner on a metal corner bead; the passage-side return face stays plain ivory. West wall (window wall) plain ivory. |
| Floor junction | 100 mm skirting cut from the Moderno Blond floor tile, ≈ 8 mm proud; every groove stops square 20 mm above it (profile end-cap). |
| Stair junction | 100 mm **stepped** skirting in the tread stone (Stairway Crema, or Black Galaxy if the granite option wins), ≈ 10 mm proud, following each tread and riser; grooves stop 20 mm above it — so each groove dies at a different height up the flight, which rhymes with the sawtooth soffit. Same on the landing edge and the terrace flight. |
| Ceiling junction | Grooves stop 20 mm below the roof-slab soffit (soft snow #F8F4EC, AP 7857 Snow Storm). |
| Panel junction | Render left FLAT for 10 mm around the walnut frame; grooves stop at the frame's top and bottom edges and resume beyond it. |

Why grooves and not the flutes render: from the sofa 5 m away, 40 mm flutes blur into fuzz, 260 mm bands with crisp slots still read as architecture; the house already has its fluted moment (teak flutes beside the TV, in the same room); flutes over a wet wall are an MDF moisture gamble; 20 mm vertical slots shed dust, 25 flutes per metre trap it; and all the boxes, conduit and driver pockets vanish inside plaster.

### 3.2 The Waveshare — a walnut-lined niche (graft from the travertine concept)

The panel is the one dark object in the sofa's prime "stair-base triangle", so it is composed *into* the comb, not stuck on it. Build it as a **box frame**: a 25 mm walnut face frame sitting 3 mm proud of the band faces, with 12 mm walnut lining boards running back into the opening to the glass, so the glass face sits ≈ 12–15 mm behind the frame (the render thickness plus the proud frame). The screen reads as set into a walnut-lined pocket — the detail you liked in the ivory-flutes render and the same "screen embedded in the wall" grammar as the foyer.

| | 10.1" (G) — current plan | 14" — under consideration |
|---|---|---|
| Masonry cavity | 280 × 195 × 80 mm, as cut | Enlarge to ≈ 350 × 235 mm (mason, **before** the render); check the real case depth |
| Frame outer | ≈ 320 × 235 mm (locked bezel size) | ≈ 400 × 280 mm |
| Window | 220 × 140 mm | per panel |
| Centre | 1500 mm above GF floor (locked) | same |
| Power | PoE+ splitter 48 V → 12 V/2 A in the cavity, on the Cat6 in C-Niche-Waveshare | PoE+ → 5 V/3 A+ USB-C splitter (check the panel's current draw) |

Set-out rule: measure the as-cut cavity centre from the east corner (expected 0.2–0.8 m) and **shift the band grid so that centre lands on a band centre**; the two end bands absorb the remainder (each ≥ 150 mm; use 9 or 11 grooves if that closes better). Foot lamp 1 goes in the same band, so "panel above lamp" is exact whatever the cavity x turns out to be.

Keep every locked detail: magnetic hatch (the frame lifts off so the unit can slide forward 150 mm on its service loops), vent slots top and bottom cut through the lining, 6 mm brass NFC dot lower-right, ACR122U behind. No 230 V on this wall → the "cavity ~200 mm above the 2M switch box" note and power option (b) in `WAVESHARE_INDOOR_PANEL.md` are void; option (a) is the only path. Ask the electrician whether a 230 V tail was left in the cavity anyway and cap it dead.

Light on the glass stays deliberately low — the accent tails put ≈ 40–60 lx there (estimate), nothing is aimed at it, and a mirror check shows no fixture reflects into the sofa's or a climber's line of sight. Run a dark dashboard theme at night so the niche reads as a dim ember, not a lamp.

**The panel size gates the plaster**: the frame outline is cast into the render. Decide 10.1" vs 14" before the mason starts; if you truly cannot, cast the flat zone at 420 × 300 and accept a flat ivory margin around a 320 mm frame.

### 3.4 Three soffit accents — Avia on a plate (graft from the gallery concept)

**What the holes are for, in plain words.** A *grazing* light sits very close to a wall and sends its light sliding *down* the wall at a shallow angle, so every ridge and slot throws a long shadow. *Lux* (lx) is how much light lands on a surface — 100 lx is a dim corridor, 300 lx a desk, 500 lx a shop. *Candela* (cd) is how strong a beam is in one direction. A *scallop* is the tongue-shaped pool a grazing light makes: bright and narrow at the top, fading and widening downward.

**The pick for ANY core size: TISVA Avia 7 W 3000 K black surface spot, rotate + tilt, CRI > 90, code `806DLA7338992`, ₹850 MRP, ×3** — mounted on a Ø130 mm × 1.5 mm matte-black powder-coated MS/aluminium cover plate screwed over each core with two Ø6 anchors; the core itself becomes the back box (driver and lead coiled inside, a grommeted Ø20 hole in the plate for the cable). Why this and not a recessed unit:

1. A core drilled square to the terrace-flight soffit points straight down or along the stair, never at the wall — only a head that rotates and tilts freely reaches the wall, and a surface head has no limit.
2. CRI > 90 (*CRI* = how truthfully colours show; 90+ is gallery grade) — the recessed TISVA candidates are Ra ≥ 80 class.
3. It fits any diameter, any depth, any ragged edge, any slope; the driver sits in free air (no heat question in a blind concrete pocket); it unscrews from a ladder on the treads for service.
4. It is the same unit as the landing light, so three black discs stepping up a soft-snow soffit read as one deliberate family with the black track and railing — not three patches.

Recessed alternatives only if you insist on a flush look after measuring the cores: Ø72–76 mm and ≥ 100 mm deep → Ivex Pro 7 W 36° COB `806DL73201ND` ₹2,590 (dimmable `806DL73201DM` ₹3,190) + Ivex recessed-tiltable black ring `T-HB46363SB` ₹1,130 (ask by code; confirm the ring tilts ≥ 30°); Ø65–68 mm and ≥ 135 mm deep → Jeno 7.5 W `8159820904` ₹3,090 (pull-out head, the only recessed unit that swings fully sideways); Ø77–90 → legacy Galaxy 7 W / Veros 9 W gimbal only if the dealer still has them; Ø100+ or < 100 mm deep → plate + Avia only. Paint any bore matte black. Keep 7 W per hole in every case.

**Where (assumed — measure):** cores above GF treads 3 / 6 / 9, x ≈ 18.6 / 20.9 / 23.2 ft (0.52 / 1.22 / 1.92 m from the corner), soffit faces ≈ 3.8 / 4.3 / 4.8 m above GF floor (≈ 3.3 m above the tread below), centres 0.3–0.5 m off the wall.

**Aim — this is the fix for the lighting judge's objection.** A vertical groove only throws a shadow if light arrives with a *sideways* component along the wall. So the beams are not pointed straight down the wall; they are thrown **down and along the wall toward the landing**:

| Core | Head rotation | Tilt from vertical | Beam centre lands (est.) | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 (over tread 3) | ≈ 50° off the wall-normal, toward the landing (west) | ≈ 25–30° | ≈ 0.5 m west of the core, ≈ 1.3 m below the soffit → (x ≈ 1.0 m, 2.5–3.0 m) | sideways throw = groove shadows; west = the chip faces away from anyone climbing and looking ahead-up |
| C2 (over tread 6) | same | ≈ 25–30° | (x ≈ 1.7 m, 3.0–3.5 m) | same |
| C3 (over tread 9) | toward the wall only (no sideways throw) | ≈ 25° | straight below, (x ≈ 1.9 m, 3.5–4.0 m) | people on the landing turn and look back up; a west-thrown C3 would put its chip in their eyes |

Light arriving along the wall at ≈ 15–20° to the surface makes a 10 mm-deep groove throw a 10–20 mm shadow — half to all of the 20 mm slot goes dark. The groove directly under a head stays bright; every groove down the tongue darkens progressively. That is the comb appearing under each spot. In afternoon daylight the west landing window does the same job for free, from the other side.

**What the light does on the wall (Avia 7 W: ≈ 520 lm and ≈ 36° beam assumed — TISVA publishes neither; peak ≈ 1,000 cd est.; all figures ± 30 %, verify with a ₹1,500 lux meter):**

- Crown ≈ 300–400 lx at 0.6–1.0 m below the soffit, ≈ 0.6 m wide → heights above GF floor ≈ 2.9–3.2 m (C1), 3.4–3.7 m (C2), 3.9–4.2 m (C3).
- ≈ 150 lx at 1.3–1.5 m below; ≈ 50 lx at 2–2.5 m below; tongue ≈ 1.2 m wide at the tail, 2–2.5 m long, no hard bottom edge.
- From the sofa: crowns 1 and 2 sit just above flight 2's nosing line, seen through the open balusters; crown 3 sits in the clear band over flight 2; the tails run down into the stair-base triangle at 40–80 lx — gentle on the screen.
- In the FULL scene the wall already carries ≈ 150 lx of borrowed ambient, so the scallops read at ≤ 3:1 — pleasant, not dramatic. The drama is the EVENING scene (pendant dimmed, accents on): then the three tongues and their shadow-lined grooves are the only bright things in the south of the room.

**Glare:** a climber on flight 1 looking *ahead-up* sees the heads from behind — black reflector, no chip. Standing directly under a core and looking straight up puts you ≈ 25–30° off the beam axis, outside the 18° half-beam — you see a lit black cone. A person on the landing looking back up at C2 is near the beam edge; if it bothers, tilt C2 2–3° less. Do a walk test (tread under each core, two treads back, landing, mid flight 2, sofa) before locking the heads. Ask the dealer whether a honeycomb louvre exists for Avia.

**Control:** own channel — a second Sonoff ZBMINI R2 (≈ ₹1,100–1,200) in the server-niche junction box on the stair circuit, no wall switch (consistent with "no switchboard"), driven from the Waveshare / HA scenes: NIGHT = foot lamps only; EVENING = foot lamps + accents + landing light; FULL = everything. Avia is on/off — if you want 40 % dimming, ask the dealer for a dimmable-driver variant or take the Ivex DM route with a Zigbee TRIAC dimmer.

**Wire route when no conduit was cast** (none was): never chase the RCC soffit — the cover to the bottom reinforcement is only ≈ 20–25 mm. Per core: if the hole centre is ≤ 250 mm from the wall, hammer-drill one Ø16–20 mm passage sideways from the bore to the wall face at the soffit–wall junction (long SDS bit, aim slightly upward so it exits above the plaster line); otherwise lay 300–500 mm of 16 mm matte-black MS conduit on the soffit surface from the plate edge to the junction (it reads as the fixture's black stem). From each junction point, 20 mm PVC conduit in a chase ≤ 30 mm deep in the **brick** wall, just under the junction line, linking the three, then one vertical chase down to the niche junction box — all under the render, all located with a pipe/cable detector first (the bath's shower riser and cistern are in the other face). 1.5 mm² FR wire.

### 3.5 The half-landing light — keep the Avia, decide its position after one tape measurement

Your "one down light… sorted" must stay an **aimable spot**, not a plain downlight: the accepted F37 is the Avia 7 W black (`806DLA7338992`, ₹850, CRI > 90) precisely because a bare-chip downlight 1.4 m over a climber's head is the 1,170 lx glare case the photometric check rejected, and because an aimed spot also lights the wall.

Measure from the landing floor to whatever is above it:

- **≈ 3.3 m (open up to the terrace half-landing, ≈ 5.1 m above GF floor) — what the stacked-flight geometry says is likely.** An Avia 7 W up there gives ≈ 40–60 lx on the wall — useless. Move it to the **west wall above the landing window head**, ≈ 4.0 m above GF floor (≈ 2.2 m above the landing), 0.3–0.5 m north of the feature wall, aimed east-and-down at the landing back wall (x ≈ 25.5 ft, 2.5 m): throw ≈ 1.6 m, ≈ 120–180 lx (est.) in a tongue ≈ 1 m wide — and because it arrives from the *side*, every groove on the landing back wall throws its full shadow. This is the one light position that genuinely suits vertical grooves (the lighting judge's favourite idea from the travertine concept). The landing floor keeps ≈ 100–150 lx from spill and bounce — comfortable for a turn. Walk-test the chip from the landing.
- **≈ 1.6 m (a slab really is there at 3.35 m).** Keep F37 on that soffit at (x 25.5 ft, y 21.25), re-aim from the old (x 23, 2.6 m) — which would now double up with C3 — to the landing back wall (x ≈ 25.5 ft, 2.5 m): tilt ≈ 50°, throw ≈ 1.5 m, pool Ø ≈ 1 m, ≈ 350 lx (est.). The landing back wall (the one fully framed rectangle the sofa sees, beside the teak window) becomes the bright, calm end of the view; the chip faces away from both flights.

Stays on the Staircase 2-way gang with the foot lamps.

### 3.6 The Axios track spot (F40)

Unchanged: Axios Pro 20 W 38° on the living track under the east edge beam, aimed at flight 2's living-facing risers (≈ 50 lx raking) with ≈ 40 lx spill on the upper wall. Its job is flight-2-as-sculpture, not the wall; the comb carries no colour, so the spill is harmless. Living gang G1.

### 3.7 The railing side

Railing stays matte-black MS with a walnut handrail on the open (north) side of flight 1 and the living side of flight 2 — **nothing on the wall side**: a wall-side handrail at 900 mm above the nosings would run straight through the panel frame. That means older users will use the wall as a rail at 0.9–1.2 m over the first treads; washable matte paint burnishes a little there and repaints for ₹2k — keep that band plain and repairable (it is). The exposed east corner gets a metal-reinforced plaster corner bead; a visible black corner guard is optional.

## 5. What it looks like

**(a) From the sofa (seated, 5 m):** a tall, calm ivory comb rising 0–5 m behind two concrete diagonals; flight 2 crosses in front (cream or black treads, risers raked by the Axios), the stair still the sculpture. Through the open balusters, three warm tongues of light climb the wall in step with the stair, each with its grooves drawn as thin dark lines that fade before the next tongue — rhythm, not a floodlit panel. Low-left, the walnut niche with its dim screen is the only dark rectangle; three small warm dots climb the flight; at the right end the landing back wall glows beside the teak window, side-lit so its grooves stand out — the eye lands there and stops. Nothing competes with the pendant cluster.

**(b) From the foyer / living NW entry (oblique, 55° off, 3.8 m):** the view is capped at ≈ 3.4 m by the 9 ft ceiling and only the east third reads: the exposed corner where plain ivory turns into grooves, the walnut niche with lamp 1 beneath it as a vertical pair, then the first flight. Oblique views foreshorten the grooves into dense dark lines — the relief is strongest from here and from the stair-base hall, and strongest of all in the afternoon when the west window rakes the whole wall.

### 6.2 Order of work (each step blocks the next)

1. Measure (§8). 2. Decide the panel size and the tread stone. 3. Plumber marks the bath-side pipe and cistern routes on this face. 4. Electrician first fix. 5. Mason: cavity enlargement if 14", straightening, corner bead, profiles, render, putty, cut joints. 6. Tiler: floor + stepped skirting (after treads). 7. Painter: primer + first coat. 8. ### 6.3 Tell the mason

"Eleven equal vertical bands and ten grooves 20 wide × 10 deep in aluminium profiles, dead plumb on laser lines, same colour as the wall. The new layer is polymer cement render 10–12 mm, not gypsum, finished with acrylic putty. Grooves stop square 20 mm above every skirting, at the panel frame, and 20 mm under the roof slab. Cut a 5 mm slot wherever the concrete flights or landing meet the brick. Metal corner bead on the exposed corner. The wall must be straight to 3 mm over 2 m — we will check it with a torch at night from the side and waviness will show. I will give you the band width from the measured length and the panel position from the measured cavity." Show him one finished groove stop before he does the rest.

### 6.4 Tell the painter

"Matte only — AP 7942 White Sand, Royale Matt, no sheen, no darker paint in the grooves. Putty primer first. Mask the groove bottoms from drips. Soft snow (AP 7857) on all concrete soffits, with the colour change exactly on the cut joint line. The final coat goes on after the lights are fitted and aimed. 2 × 2 ft sample on the real wall, judged morning, afternoon and evening."

### 6.5 Tell the carpenter

"A walnut box frame, not a flat bezel: 25 mm face frame 3 mm proud of the plaster, 12 mm lining boards running back to the glass, matte PU, colour #4A3526 like the handrail, mitred, horizontal grain. Outer 320 × 235 for the 10.1-inch or 400 × 280 for the 14-inch — build it around the real screen once it is here; the plaster is already flat under it and the grooves stop at its edges, so its outline cannot move. Magnetic catches so the whole unit lifts off and slides forward 150 mm; vent slots top and bottom through the lining; 6 mm brass dot lower-right."

### 6.6 Tell the electrician

"No switch or 230 V box on this wall — the panel gets 12 V from a PoE+ splitter on its Cat6; cap any mains tail in the cavity. Three foot-lamp boxes (45 mm round) on the band centre lines 300 mm above treads 2, 6 and 10, faces 10–12 mm below the cement plaster, on a 20 mm loop to the staircase 2-way. Three accent spots on a SEPARATE channel: a second ZBMINI R2 in the niche junction box. For each core: drill a Ø16–20 passage sideways from the hole to the wall at the soffit line if the hole is within 250 mm of the wall, otherwise a short black 16 mm MS conduit on the soffit surface; then 20 mm PVC in a chase no deeper than 30 mm in the brick — never in the concrete steps or soffit — joining the three and dropping to the niche box, after the plumber has marked the bath pipes. Fit a Ø130 black plate over each core, mount the Avia on it, coil the driver inside the hole. 7 W only. Aim: cores 1 and 2 turned toward the landing and tilted 25–30°, core 3 toward the wall only; walk test from the treads, the landing, flight 2 and the sofa before locking. The landing light stays the Avia — re-aim or move it per the measured ceiling height."

### 6.7 Ask the TISVA dealer before paying

Avia base-plate diameter, tilt/rotate range, lumens, and whether a dimmable driver exists; CRI 90 availability for the Spotlit; whether a honeycomb louvre fits the Avia; and, only if a recessed look is wanted, the Ivex tiltable ring `T-HB46363` availability and tilt angle.

## 7. Grafts taken, and what was rejected

**Taken**

- From the travertine + walnut concept: the walnut-lined recessed niche for the Waveshare (coherence judge's graft — it is the flutes-render detail you liked and the foyer's embedded-screen grammar), and the west-wall side-light for the landing if it is open to 5.1 m (lighting judge: "the one idea that genuinely suits vertical relief").
- From the linen-gallery concept: the Avia 7 W on a black plate over each core with the driver in the bore and its own ZBMINI channel (buildability and lighting judges both grafted it — CRI > 90, fits any core, serviceable from a ladder, ₹4k instead of ₹13k), the picture-light rule for the landing wall, and the rule that if art ever goes on this wall it is one unglazed vertical piece on the landing back wall and nothing else.
- Corrections to the Comb itself from the judges: beams thrown along the wall (vertical grooves need sideways light); polymer cement render instead of gypsum over the bath wall; cut joints on the RCC lines; the first crown placed where the sofa can see it; the landing ceiling treated as probably 5.1 m.

**Rejected, and why**

- Travertine field: a third stone wall within metres of the foyer ledgestone and the TV-wall stone in the one space the palette allows one feature wall; three near-match creams stacked at the stair base; ₹1.35–1.6 L; lot-matching with Bed 2; re-sealing for life.
- The 0.9 × 3.35 m dark-walnut column: a dark mass in the sofa's nearest, brightest corner that swallows accent 1 at night.
- Textured vinyl wallcovering: 6.5 m drops on the back of a shower/cistern wall, seams and a wrapped corner at shoulder height, no local installer, full re-drop to repair.
- The Lambani textile hero over treads 8–10: 35 mm projection at head height, afternoon sun, dust, and mirror-work glints into every seat; the pichwai/Lambani piece belongs on the stair-base hall wall opposite, as the decor report also allows.
- The Comb's own Ivex recessed modules with drivers buried in Ø65 pockets under the finished plaster (a dead driver would mean cutting the feature); the straight-down aim; a plain downlight over the landing; step strips; a wall-side handrail.
